# Comment on Tagging Article

**Original Article**: [A Deep Dive into Standardized Tagging](https://stream-perfect.blogspot.com/2025/04/a-deep-dive-into-standardized-tagging.html)

**Comment by Frank Robinson**

Great article, concise and informative.

I would like to see a bit of discussion on the life cycle of tags. I'm fairly sure this article is written from the point of view of someone in an operating facility, as it mostly refers to the need to tag assets. There is a common grey area here, as most tags originate in the design phase of an engineering project. At this stage, there is no asset. The asset is only manifest after purchase, or at least during the purchase process. The assignment of assets to tags is a key issue in asset management and maintenance.

For example, I have a water distribution pump and our operator needs to be able to check the discharge pressure, so I have added "J:PG-1005 Water distribution pump discharge" to my design. We produce a data sheet specifying the requirements for this gauge, and eventually buy it, probably along with all the other pressure gauges on the project for cost benefit. 

If our project is well funded, we could order it with tag attached, whether it be a stainless steel tag, a QR code sticker, RFID device, whatever. If we are not so well off we have to do that part ourselves. We should also know or be able to allocate a purchase price, and have the other information required by asset management. 

By applying a physical tag of some sort, we have assigned the asset purchased to the design tag. But what about when a clumsy mechanic wielding a large iron tool manages to smash our delicate instrument? It happens! So now, we need a new pressure gauge. Either we have some spares with the right range and can allocate a replacement, or we need to buy it again. The asset register needs to be updated with the sad facts of the demise of our instrument, but the information should not be deleted. A new pressure gauge needs to be added to the asset register. What is its unique tag? What was the unique tag of the asset waiting on the store room shelf, slowly rusting (should have specified stainless)?

I'm trying to highlight that the design tag and the asset tag are different things with different life cycles. The asset tag doesn't need to follow the same tagging system as the design, though there are probably benefits if it does.

I don't have recent experience with asset or maintenance systems, and I'm very interested to hear if there is a "most common approach" taken by the more popular systems.
